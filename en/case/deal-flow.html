01 context

Whoever books the trip faster wins

A platform for booking freight: a manager takes the request, picks a carrier, agrees the rate and closes the deal. That path used to stretch across eleven screens, half of which asked for the same data twice.

The problem was not that the interface was complex — it was that it did not remember context. The manager typed in what the system already knew.

02 constraints

The backend stays as it is

Field order in the API is fixed — the sequence of steps can only change within the endpoints that already exist.

No user training

60 managers work a shift without a break. The release could not demand training.

13-inch laptops

70% of sessions come from small screens. Every permanent element costs rows of the table.

03 process

Watching a shift, not interviewing

Six shifts, three hours each, sitting beside the managers. We recorded actions rather than opinions: how often someone goes back, where they keep data in a notebook, when they switch to a messenger.

It turned out 4 of the 11 steps existed only to confirm what the manager had already seen on the previous screen.

04 key decisions

Three forks

fork

Filters would not fit in the table header

options

A. Side panelB. Modal windowC. A separate step in the wizard

what I chose

B — 70% of sessions come from a 13-inch laptop, and a side panel ate a third of the table. A modal opens from the keyboard and does not change the width of the data.

fork

Where to put the four confirmation steps

options

A. Remove them entirelyB. One combined confirmationC. Inline editing

what I chose

B — a deal runs to €40k and managers asked for a point to stop at before sending. Removing everything would have shifted the risk onto a person with no safety net.

fork

The rate depends on six parameters

options

A. A single numberB. A breakdown by rowsC. A number plus details on demand

what I chose

C — the manager needs a number for the phone call, but in 1 case out of 5 the client asks why it costs that. The details have to sit one click away, not on screen at all times.

A month after release, support stopped getting the question of where to see the rate — before that it was a third of all tickets.

06 reflection

One thing I would do differently: start with the logs, not the observations. Three weeks in the field confirmed what analytics would have shown in a day — and only after that would watching people have made sense as a way to understand why.
